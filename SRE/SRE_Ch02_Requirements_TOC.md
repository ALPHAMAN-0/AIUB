## 1. The Expectation Gap
**Slide 2**

- Illustrates the mismatch between what the customer actually needs and what tends to get delivered
- This gap is the core motivation for doing requirements engineering carefully

## 2. Stakeholders
**Slide 3**

- **Definition:** a person, group, or organization that is actively involved in a project, is affected by its process or outcome, or can influence its process or outcome
- Stakeholders can be **internal or external** to the project team and to the developing organization

## 3. Who Is the Customer?
**Slide 4**

- Customers are a **subset of stakeholders**
- **Definition:** an individual or organization that derives direct or indirect benefit from a product
- Customers may **request, pay for, select, specify, use, or receive** a product's output
- **Users** are a subset of customers — the people requirements should ultimately come from
  - **Direct users** — operate the product hands-on
  - **Indirect users** — receive outputs without touching the system (e.g., a manager receiving an automated report)
- Users can describe the **tasks** they perform, the **outputs** they need, and the **quality** they expect

## 4. The Customer–Development Partnership
**Slides 5–6**

**Customer Rights** — what customers can expect from BAs/developers:
- BAs speak your language and learn your business
- Receive explanations of requirements practices and deliverables
- Change your requirements, in an environment of mutual respect
- Hear ideas and alternatives; describe what makes the product easy to use
- Learn ways to accelerate development through reuse
- Receive a system that meets your functional and quality needs

**Customer Responsibilities** — what customers owe BAs/developers in return:
- Educate BAs/developers about your business
- Dedicate time to provide and clarify requirements
- Be specific and precise; make timely decisions
- Respect a developer's cost/feasibility assessments
- Set realistic priorities in collaboration with developers
- Review requirements and evaluate prototypes
- Establish acceptance criteria; communicate changes promptly
- Respect the requirements development process (SDLC)

## 5. Identifying Decision Makers
**Slide 7**

- Four decision-making models:
  - The leader decides (with or without discussion)
  - Majority vote
  - Unanimous vote
  - Consensus (everyone can live with it)
- **Reaching agreement on requirements** — from each stakeholder's view:
  - Customers: needs are addressed
  - Developers: requirements are understood and feasible
  - Testers: requirements are verifiable
  - Management: business objectives will be achieved

## 6. The Requirements Baseline
**Slide 8**

- **Definition:** a set of requirements that has been reviewed and agreed upon, serving as the basis for further development
- Baselining builds confidence for:
  - Customer management/marketing — scope won't explode
  - User representatives — the right solution will be delivered
  - Development management — a focused business partner exists
  - BAs/project managers — changes can be managed without chaos
  - QA/test teams — test scripts can be developed confidently

## 7. The Business Analyst (BA) Role
**Slide 9**

- Positions the BA as the **connecting link** between business/customer-side stakeholders (sponsors, user reps, other stakeholders) and technical/delivery-side stakeholders (project management, development, testing)

## 8. The Business Analyst's Tasks
**Slide 10**

1. Define business requirements
2. Plan the requirements approach
3. Identify project stakeholders and user classes
4. Elicit requirements
5. Analyse requirements
6. Document requirements
7. Communicate requirements
8. Lead requirements validation
9. Facilitate requirements prioritization
10. Manage requirements

## 9. Essential Analyst Skills
**Slide 11**

- Listening skills
- Interviewing and questioning skills
- Thinking on your feet
- Analytical skills
- Systems thinking skills
- Learning skills
- Facilitation / simplification skills
- Leadership skills
- Observational skills
- Communication skills
- Organizational skills
- Modelling skills
- Interpersonal skills
- Creativity

## 10. Essential Analyst Knowledge
**Slide 12**

- A breadth of knowledge, much of it gained through experience
- Understanding contemporary requirements engineering practices and how to apply them across various software development life cycles

## 11. The Making of a Business Analyst
**Slide 13**

Common paths into the BA role:
- The former user
- The former developer or tester
- The former (or concurrent) project manager
- The subject matter (domain) expert

*Knowledge + Skills + Experience → practising the tasks → a well-rounded BA*
